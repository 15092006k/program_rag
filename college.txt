30. RAG PROJECT

A college information RAG application can use this document as
its knowledge source.

The first step is document loading. A text loader reads
college.txt and creates a document object.

The second step is text splitting. A text splitter divides the
large document into smaller chunks.

The third step is embedding generation. Each chunk is converted
into a numerical vector representing its semantic meaning.

The fourth step is vector storage. The embeddings and associated
text are stored in a Chroma vector database.

The fifth step is similarity search. When a user asks a question,
the question is converted into an embedding and compared with
stored document embeddings.

The sixth step is Top-K retrieval. The system selects the most
relevant K chunks.

The seventh step is context construction. The retrieved chunks
are combined into a context.

The final step is generation. A language model uses the retrieved
context to create a natural-language answer.

Question: What does the placement cell do?

Answer: The placement cell organizes career training, aptitude

sessions, and campus recruitment activities.

Question: What is RAG?

Answer: Retrieval-Augmented Generation retrieves relevant
information from a knowledge source and provides that information
as context to a language model before generating an answer.

Question: Why is text splitting needed?

Answer: Large documents are divided into smaller chunks so that
relevant sections can be efficiently converted into embeddings
and retrieved during a search.

Question: What are embeddings?

Answer: Embeddings are numerical representations of text that
capture semantic meaning and allow similar pieces of text to be
compared mathematically.

Question: What is Chroma?

Answer: Chroma is a vector database that stores embeddings and
associated documents and supports similarity-based retrieval.

Question: What is Top-K retrieval?

Answer: Top-K retrieval means selecting the K most relevant
document chunks for a user's query.

Question: Why is context construction important?

Answer: Context construction combines relevant retrieved chunks
into an information block that can be provided to a language model.
